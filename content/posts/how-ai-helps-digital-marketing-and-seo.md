<p>Last month I watched a business owner spend forty minutes writing one follow-up email to a lead. It was a good email. It was also the third time that week he'd written roughly the same one from scratch. Meanwhile, an AI could have drafted it in four seconds — and he'd have spent his forty minutes on the two enquiries he never got round to answering at all.</p>

<p>That, in one scene, is the real story of AI in digital marketing. It's not magic and it's not a threat. It's a very fast junior assistant that never sleeps — brilliant at some jobs, dangerous at others, and completely dependent on someone who knows the difference.</p>

<p>Having spent five-plus years running Facebook campaigns and now building AI systems for a living, here's my honest map of where that line sits in 2026.</p>

<h2>Where AI genuinely earns its keep in marketing</h2>

<h3>1. Speed to lead — the biggest win nobody talks about</h3>
<p>The most valuable thing AI can do for a service business isn't writing clever ads. It's answering enquiries while your competitors are asleep. A lead that gets a thoughtful reply in sixty seconds converts at a completely different rate to one that waits four hours — I've written about <a href="/blog/why-your-facebook-leads-go-cold">why leads go cold so quickly</a>, and it's the first place I'd point any business owner. AI drafts the reply instantly; a human approves it with one tap. Speed of a machine, judgement of a person.</p>

<h3>2. First drafts, at scale</h3>
<p>Ad variations, email subject lines, product descriptions, social captions — AI produces workable first drafts in seconds. The key word is <em>first</em>. The businesses getting results treat AI output as clay, not sculpture: it saves the blank-page hour, then a human adds the detail only they know — the real prices, the actual delivery times, the thing customers always ask on the phone.</p>

<h3>3. Reading your own data</h3>
<p>Most small businesses sit on data they never look at: which ads actually led to bookings, which enquiry sources go quiet, what people ask before they buy. AI is genuinely good at summarising a messy month of numbers into "your weekend enquiries convert at half the rate because nobody replies until Monday". That sentence is worth more than any ad copy.</p>

<h3>4. Testing velocity</h3>
<p>Meta's ad system is itself AI, and it rewards being fed. More creative variations, tested faster, means the algorithm finds your winners sooner. AI-assisted creative production means a small business can test like a big one — ten angles a week instead of one.</p>

<h2>What's happening to Google SEO — the honest version</h2>

<p>SEO is changing faster right now than at any point I've worked in marketing, and most of what's written about it is either panic or sales copy. Three things are actually true:</p>

<p><strong>First: AI answers are eating simple searches.</strong> When someone asks "what is the Meta pixel", Google's AI overview answers it on the results page. Generic definition-style content is dying — the click never happens. What still earns visits is content an AI summary can't replace: real experience, specific numbers, opinions with reasons, local knowledge.</p>

<p><strong>Second: Google explicitly penalises mass-produced AI content.</strong> Its spam policies name "scaled content abuse" — churning out pages that add nothing. Publishing thirty AI-written posts a week isn't an SEO strategy; it's a slow-motion penalty. The irony is that AI-<em>assisted</em> content written with genuine expertise is fine, and always has been. Google's own guidance cares about whether content is helpful, not whether a machine touched it.</p>

<p><strong>Third: being cited by AI is becoming its own channel.</strong> ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI features all recommend businesses. They favour sites with clear structured data, plain-language answers to real questions, and consistent facts about who you are and what you do. Optimising for that — some call it answer engine optimisation — mostly looks like classic good practice done properly: schema markup, honest FAQ pages, fast pages, specifics over slogans.</p>

<h2>Where humans still win, comfortably</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Knowing what's true.</strong> AI will confidently invent statistics, features you don't offer, and prices you never agreed. Every AI-drafted message to a customer needs a human gate. In our own systems this is a hard rule: nothing sends without approval.</li>
<li><strong>Strategy.</strong> AI can write a hundred ads for a broken offer. It cannot tell you your offer is broken. Choosing the niche, the price, the promise — still a human job.</li>
<li><strong>Experience.</strong> Google's quality guidance leans heavily on first-hand experience, and readers can smell its absence. The catering owner who writes about what actually goes wrong at a 200-guest wedding will outrank a thousand generic "event planning tips" pages.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Five things you can do this week (without hiring anyone)</h2>

<ol>
<li>Paste your last ten customer enquiries into an AI chat and ask what questions come up repeatedly — then answer them on your website. That's real SEO content.</li>
<li>Write one master reply template with AI, edit it until it sounds like you, and reuse it for every new enquiry.</li>
<li>Ask AI to turn your best customer conversation into a case study draft. Add the real numbers yourself.</li>
<li>Check your ad account's last ninety days with fresh eyes: which campaigns produced enquiries that actually answered the phone? Kill the rest.</li>
<li>Add FAQ structured data to your site so both Google and AI assistants can quote you accurately.</li>
</ol>

AI does the speed. You keep the judgement. That combination — not either half alone — is what's actually winning right now.</p>
