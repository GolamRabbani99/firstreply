<p>Of all the AI success stories doing the rounds, this one deserves your attention precisely because it's small. No robot armies, no billion-pound lab. Just a salesperson who was sick of answering emails at midnight.</p>

<p>In <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n4ZxEznNaIY" rel="noopener">a two-minute video from Anthropic's Claude channel</a>, Jared Sires — a go-to-market product manager who started as an account executive with <strong>zero coding experience</strong> — explains the problem: sales reps drown in administrative work, digging through scattered documentation and answering customer emails late into the night.</p>

<p>His fix: he built <strong>CLAFTS</strong> ("Claude Drafts"), an AI email assistant that drafts customer replies <em>in his own voice</em>. Not a generic autoresponder — a system trained on how he actually writes, doing the first 80% of every reply so he only does the judgement part.</p>

<h2>Three things worth stealing from this story</h2>

<h3>1. The best automation ideas come from the person doing the job</h3>
<p>Nobody in a strategy meeting would have specced CLAFTS. It exists because the person feeling the pain built the painkiller. In your business, the person who knows what to automate isn't a consultant — it's whoever answers the enquiries, chases invoices or writes the same quote email eleven times a week. Ask them what they'd kill first.</p>

<h3>2. "In my own voice" is the whole game</h3>
<p>Customers can smell a template. The reason this assistant works is that its drafts sound like Jared, not like a robot doing an impression of customer service. Any AI reply system worth having learns your phrasing, your sign-off, the way you actually talk — and then a human still reads it before it sends.</p>

<h3>3. The barrier to entry has collapsed</h3>
<p>A man with no coding background built a working AI tool for his own workflow. The excuse "we're not a tech company" is now officially retired. The realistic question isn't <em>can</em> a small business have this — it's whether you build it yourself over some weekends or have someone wire it up for you in a fortnight.</p>
